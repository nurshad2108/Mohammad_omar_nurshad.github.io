Dengue thesis

“Modeling and Predicting Daily Dengue Cases in Bangladesh Using Meteorological Factors: A Comparative Study of Statistical and Machine-Learning Approaches”
M.S. thesis, Department of Statistics, University of Chittagong, September 2026. Supervisor: Prof. Mohammad Salim Zahangir. Sole author.

Question and gap

Many dengue–climate studies use monthly data, cover one city, ignore overdispersion, or evaluate forecasts with information leakage. This thesis uses daily, all-division data and keeps explaining separate from predicting.

Design

Track A (association): DGHS case counts linked to BMD weather for 2022–2024; NB2 models with lag comparison, nonlinear terms and ZINB, GEE and PPML sensitivity checks. Track B (forecasting): leakage-controlled 7-day-ahead models validated on rolling origins and a held-out 2025 test.

Findings

  • Overdispersion was large (Pearson dispersion 30.45 under the baseline, about 1.14 under NB2).
  • A 28-day lag had the lowest AIC; a 14-day lag was close (ΔAIC 1.50), so no single delay is identified.
  • Temperature was the one robust weather association: nonlinear, delayed, ecological and not causal.
  • Recent case history dominated the forecasts. Peaks were underpredicted (P95+ MAE 84.86) and intervals undercovered.

Limitations

Ecological design (no individual or causal claims); the 2025 test is temporal within the same surveillance system, not prospective or external; upstream data provenance is incomplete.

My contribution

I designed the study and carried out the data linkage, modelling and validation under Prof. Zahangir’s supervision.

Access

Surveillance (DGHS) and weather (BMD) records are not redistributed here. No public code repository is linked yet.

Fast-food preprint

“Sedentary Behavior and Eating Habits Associated with Fast Food Consumption Among Bangladeshi University Students: The Moderating Role of Residential Status”
Md. Abu Faisal, Mohammad Omar Nurshad, Md. Taufiqul Alam, Anwoy Barua. Preprint, not peer reviewed

medRxiv, posted 28 April 2026. DOI 10.64898/2026.04.24.26351719. The manuscript is under revision at PLOS ONE (PONE-D-26-19727) and stays listed as a preprint until a published version exists.

Design and methods

Cross-sectional paper survey of 433 University of Chittagong students aged 18–25 (2025, 50-student pilot, ethics approval reported). Chi-square and Fisher’s exact tests; proportional-odds ordinal regression with residential-status interactions; Brant test.

Findings (associations only)

  • 91.9% ate fast food weekly; 74.6% more than three days a week.
  • Higher odds: snacking >3 days/week (OR 1.77–2.17), eating while watching TV/videos very often (OR 2.68), commuting over 30 minutes.
  • Lower odds: home-cooked meals as the main source (OR 0.32).
  • Emotional eating × rented accommodation: OR 1.28 (95% CI 1.02–1.60, p = 0.034).

My contribution

Conceptualization, investigation, statistical analysis, data curation, original draft, and review and editing.

Limitations and data

Cross-sectional (no causal inference), single university, convenience sampling, self-report, modest composite-score reliability (α 0.576–0.637). The data statement lists a de-identified dataset on Figshare (doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.32025564).
